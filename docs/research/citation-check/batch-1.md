### 1. Alkoby et al. 2018
- **Status:** OK (one figure not checked)
- **Citation:** Alkoby O, Abu-Rmileh A, Shriki O, Todder D (2018). Can we predict who will respond to neurofeedback? A review of the inefficacy problem and existing predictors for successful EEG neurofeedback learning. *Neuroscience* 378:155–164.

### 2. Allen, Harmon-Jones, Cavender 2001
- **Status:** CORRECT-METADATA
- **Citation:** Allen JJB, Harmon-Jones E, Cavender JH (2001). Manipulation of frontal EEG asymmetry through biofeedback alters self-reported emotional responses **and facial EMG**. *Psychophysiology* 38(4):685–693.
- **DOI/URL:** 10.1017/S0048577201991255 (Wiley form: 10.1111/1469-8986.3840685)
- **Claim check (l.323, "trained asymmetry and shifted mood"):** Partly supported. 18 right-handed women had 5 days of F3/F4 asymmetry biofeedback, 9 per direction. Asymmetry changed in the trained direction. What changed afterwards was self-reported affect and facial EMG *in response to emotional film clips*, not resting mood.
- **Fix:** Add "and facial EMG" to the title. Reword to "shifted self-reported emotional responses to film clips (n=18)". Grade C is right.

### 3. Balban et al. 2023
- **Status:** OK
- **Citation:** Balban MY, Neri E, Kogon MM, Weed L, Nouriani B, Jo B, Holl G, Zeitzer JM, Spiegel D, Huberman AD (2023). Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal. *Cell Reports Medicine* 4(1):100895.
- **DOI/URL:** 10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895 · PMID 36630953
- **Claim check (l.150):** Yes. 108 people were randomized to one of four 5-minute daily practices for 28 days. Cyclic sighing gave the largest daily rise in positive affect and the largest drop in respiratory rate. Both were significantly greater than mindfulness meditation. EEG was not measured (WHOOP wristband only).
- **Fix:** None needed. Optionally add "(n=108, 28 days, remote RCT)". "Grade B" is generous for one remote RCT; B–C is fairer.

### 5. Benedek et al. 2014
- **Status:** OK
- **Citation:** Benedek M, Schickel RJ, Jauk E, Fink A, Neubauer AC (2014). Alpha power increases in right parietal cortex reflects focused internal attention. *Neuropsychologia* 56:393–400.
- **DOI/URL:** 10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2014.02.010 · PMC3989020
- **Claim check (l.171, "alpha rises during internally focused creative ideation"):** Partly supported. 40 students did two tasks, each with the stimulus either visible or masked. The divergent-thinking task (alternate uses) showed high alpha whether or not the stimulus was masked. Masking raised alpha only in the sentence task. The authors read right-parietal alpha as task-focused internal attention or "task shielding", not creativity as such.
- **Fix:** "Benedek et al. 2014 found right-parietal alpha rises with focused internal attention, including during creative idea generation."

### 9. Garcia-Argibay, Santed, Reales 2019

### 12. Haar Horowitz et al. 2020
- **Status:** CORRECT-METADATA / partly wrong claim
- **Citation:** Haar Horowitz A, Cunningham TJ, Maes P, Stickgold R (2020). Dormio: a targeted dream incubation device. *Consciousness and Cognition* 83:102938.
- **DOI/URL:** 10.1016/j.concog.2020.102938 · PMC7590944
- **Claim check (l.267, "deliberately hold people near this edge"):** Partly supported. Dormio uses no EEG. It detects sleep onset from hand-worn sensors (heart rate, finger flexion, EDA). After each onset it waits 1–5 minutes, wakes the person, collects a dream report, plays a prompt, and lets them fall asleep again, repeating over a 45-minute nap (n=49). It cycles people in and out of hypnagogia rather than holding them at the edge.
- **Fix:** "...the MIT Dormio work (Haar Horowitz et al. 2020) repeatedly returns people to the hypnagogic edge, detecting sleep onset from hand-worn heart-rate, EDA and finger-flexion sensors and waking them with a prompt." This also bears on the owner's question about detecting sleep onset: Dormio shows non-EEG signals can be used.

### 13. Hardt & Kamiya 1978
- **Status:** CLAIM-WRONG (as attributed at l.298)
- **Citation:** Hardt JV, Kamiya J (1978). Anxiety change through electroencephalographic alpha feedback seen only in high anxiety subjects. *Science* 201(4350):79–81.
- **DOI/URL:** 10.1126/science.663641 · PMID 663641
- **Claim check:**
  - l.87, "people can learn to raise alpha": Yes.
  - l.298, "effects on anxiety are real but overlap with relaxation and placebo (Hardt and Kamiya 1978...)": Partly wrong. In high-trait-anxiety subjects, anxiety fell when alpha rose and rose when alpha was suppressed. That change did not track frontalis EMG or respiration rate, and the authors argue for an alpha-specific effect. Low-anxiety subjects controlled alpha better, but their anxiety did not change.
- **Fix:** "alpha increase lowered anxiety only in high-trait-anxiety subjects, independent of EMG and respiration (Hardt and Kamiya 1978); later reviews suggest much of the effect overlaps with relaxation and placebo." Grade B–C.

### 16. Hove et al. 2016
- **Status:** CLAIM-WRONG
- **Citation:** Hove MJ, Stelzer J, Nierhaus T, Thiel SD, Gundlach C, Margulies DS, Van Dijk KRA, Turner R, Keller PE, Merker B (2016). Brain network reconfiguration and perceptual decoupling during an absorptive state of consciousness. *Cerebral Cortex* 26(7):3116–3124.
- **DOI/URL:** 10.1093/cercor/bhv137 · PMID 26108612 · PMC4898667
- **Claim check (l.239, "found network changes but not simple theta entrainment"):** Misleading. This was an **fMRI** study of 15 experienced shamanic practitioners and recorded no EEG, so it could not test for theta entrainment. During trance it found higher eigenvector centrality in posterior cingulate, dorsal ACC and left insula/operculum, and reduced connectivity of auditory-pathway seeds (perceptual decoupling).
- **Fix:** "Hove et al. (2016, fMRI, n=15 practitioners) found default-mode and control-network reconfiguration and decoupling of auditory pathways during drumming-induced trance; EEG entrainment was not measured." Grade C.
