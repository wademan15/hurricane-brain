## Athena packets

| Fact | Source | Verified against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notification = packets back to back; byte 0 = packet length incl. header; byte 1 packet counter; 14-byte header; first subpacket's tag at byte 9, counter at 10; then `[tag][counter][3 unknown][payload]` | muse-rs `parse.rs` + `docs/athena-protocol.md`; OpenMuse `decode.py`; muse-lsl/BrainFlow `athena.py` | real captures: 9 fixtures, 4,663 subpackets, identical to both reference decoders |
| Payload sizes: 0x11/0x12 28 B, 0x34 30, 0x35 40, 0x36 40, 0x47 36, 0x53 24, 0x98 20, 0x88 to end of packet | muse-rs; OpenMuse (0x88 minimum 188 B) | captures; one fw 3.1.19 packet has a 151-byte 0x88 that OpenMuse skips and muse-rs reads; we read it (test covers it) |
| EEG 14-bit LSB-first, sample-major; 0x11 = 4 ch × 4 samples, 0x12 = 8 ch × 2 | all three | captures (exact raw integers) |
| EEG µV = (raw − 8192) × 1450/16383 | OpenMuse (scale, no offset); muse-lsl/BrainFlow (scale and offset); muse-rs ((raw − 8192) × 0.0885) | scale **not checked against a known input voltage**; raw integers identical to muse-rs; our µV differ from muse-rs by 0.007% (its rounded scale) |
| Optics 20-bit LSB-first, sample-major, unscaled | all three (OpenMuse divides by 32768 for display) | captures |
| Optical channel names (LO/RO/LI/RI × NIR/IR/RED/AMB); 4-ch mode = canonical 4..7 | OpenMuse `decode.py` (validated there by cross-correlation); muse-lsl canonical index mapping | **unverified by us**; muse-rs names them differently |
| IMU: 18 × i16 LE, 3 samples × (acc xyz, gyro xyz); gyro scale negated (−0.0074768) | all three | captures |
| Battery: u16 LE / 256 = % (0x88 and 0x98) | muse-rs (checked against status `bp`), OpenMuse (tests at known levels) | captures at 16.80 %, 58.27 %, 90.40 % decode within 0.5 %. muse-lsl uses /512 and is contradicted by these captures |
| Per-tag 8-bit counter, +1 per subpacket, independent of the packet counter | muse-rs (measured: EEG counter skips about 1 in 140 subpackets on hardware; packet counter never skips); OpenMuse | captures: every tag advances by exactly 1 in the OpenMuse captures; muse-rs capture has two real skips (optics 107→110, IMU 30→32) which our pipeline reports as gaps of 2 and 3 samples |
| Header bytes 2-5 as a device clock | OpenMuse: u32 LE at 256 kHz; muse-rs: does not fit fw 3.1.19 | **disputed**; not used |
| Channels 5-8 of 8-channel EEG | vendor page (via OpenMuse README): "4 amplified Aux channels"; OpenMuse AUX_1..4; muse-rs FPz, AUX_R, AUX_L, AUX (no source given) | named AUX1..AUX4; physical meaning **unverified** |
| EEG rate 256 Hz | all sources | OpenMuse captures: 15,518-23,232 samples over 60-90 s of arrival times give 256.4-256.7 Hz, consistent with 256 within the start/end latency of a 60 s span |

Firmware note: every Athena capture and measurement available was made on firmware 3.1.x running
on Athena hardware (`Athena_RevE`/`RevF`), not "firmware 4.x". muse-rs's comments say "fw ≥ 4.x"
but its measurements are on 3.1.11 and 3.1.19. Whether firmware 4.x changes anything is unknown.

## Design decisions recorded here

- **Decode in TypeScript, not Rust.** One implementation, used live and in replay, testable with
  vitest. No `crates/hb-device` was created.
- **Clock.** EEG index from counters only while the link is up. Arrival time is used only to (1)
  map host time onto session time for chest strap beats, (2) choose between wrap candidates when
  a loss is longer than the counter range and to place the stream after a reconnect or counter
  restart (those gaps are flagged `estimated`), (3) trim the rate of optical and motion streams,
  which may run on their own oscillators, continuously and without jumps. See `clock.ts`.
- **Gaps.** Classic packets whose four channels do not all arrive within 3 packets are released
  as a 12-sample gap (`reassembly`). Athena counter skips are gaps of `missing × samples per
  subpacket`. Overlaps (only possible after an estimated resync) are trimmed, never emitted twice.
- **Stream description** is declared from what actually arrives in the first 512 EEG samples
  (2 s): channel count from the EEG tag, optics only if optics arrived. Data before it is queued,
  not dropped. A stream that starts later than that is not declared and its data is dropped.
- **Arrival times** are integer microseconds since a shared host origin, quantized before decoding,
  so a recording replays bit for bit.

1. No capture from a **Classic** headset (Muse 2, Muse S classic firmware) was available. Classic
   decoding is verified against muse-js's test vectors and the agreement of muse-js, muse-rs and
   muse-lsl code, not against a recorded session. First task with the owner's Muse 2: record a
   session with `Recorder` and add it as a fixture.

   step, the exact PPG channel order (ambient/infrared/red from muse-js comments), and whether p50
   is right for Muse S on classic firmware.
3. Athena firmware 4.x: no data. Every Athena fact above is from firmware 3.1.x.
4. Athena EEG scale in absolute µV, the meaning of channels 5-8, the optical channel roles, the
   0x53 DRL/REF block (decoded raw, not passed on), the rest of the 0x88 block, the header clock.
5. Whether the 0x0014 characteristic ever carries data.
6. Whether keep-alive `k` is needed; what `L1` does (LED per muse-rs, low-latency per OpenMuse).
7. Real Web Bluetooth behaviour on the owner's machines (Windows 11 Chrome, Android Chrome):
   connection interval, notification bursts, Android permission prompts. Tested only with a fake
   GATT stack in `test/fakeBluetooth.ts`.
8. The rate trim of optical/motion clocks is tested on synthetic streams (0.5% fast) only.
